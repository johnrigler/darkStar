# Dark Star: Carolyn Fowler / Desert Wake Notes

This document preserves the developing structure of the Carolyn Fowler movement in **Dark Star**.

## Finnegans Wake in the desert

The Carolyn Fowler section should draw heavily on the **Washers at the Ford** material from *Finnegans Wake*, but translate its geography into western New Mexico.

Instead of women speaking across or beside a river, the scene is organized around the **Continental Divide**. The Divide takes over the structural role of the river: a boundary that separates while also defining a shared landscape.

This is **Finnegans Wake in the desert**.

The transformation matters. The Wake's water imagery is not simply copied into New Mexico. River becomes watershed. Flow becomes division. The point where water chooses one continental direction or another becomes a literal and symbolic conversational boundary.

The women on either side can speak across that divide while carrying Navajo, Laguna, and Acoma perspectives, languages, misunderstandings, jokes, histories, and local arguments. The geography itself becomes part of the syntax.

## The women and the Washers at the Ford

The Navajo women in particular draw heavily on the Washers at the Ford.

The model is not a direct character substitution. It is a structural borrowing: women talking, gossiping, arguing, remembering, naming, correcting, and transforming the world through conversation while a geographic boundary runs between them.

In Dark Star the boundary is not a ford. It is the Continental Divide.

That substitution changes the scale. A ford is a local crossing in a river. The Continental Divide is an enormous hydrological abstraction made physically present in the landscape. It divides flows that eventually reach different oceans.

That makes it appropriate to Dark Star's larger concerns with routing, networks, forks, competing systems, and the strange consequences of choosing one path rather than another.

## Four avunculars

The **four avunculars** from the Wake are displaced into the landscape as a **rock formation near Acoma**.

They should not necessarily be introduced as four named men. The reader can first encounter them as terrain, silhouettes, landmarks, or apparently ordinary geological features. Their identity as the four avunculars can emerge gradually or remain only partly legible.

This continues a basic Dark Star operation: a literary or mythic figure can become geography, artifact, animal, code, machine-readable object, or some other material thing without a clean transition explaining when metaphor became literal.

The four avunculars therefore become part of the land itself.

## Structural principle

The Carolyn Fowler movement is not merely a story set in New Mexico with Joyce references placed on top of it. It should behave as though *Finnegans Wake* has been rehydrated under desert conditions.

The substitutions are functional:

- river / ford → Continental Divide
- washers → Navajo women and neighboring women in conversation
- flowing water → watershed and directional split
- Dublin geography → Grants / Acoma / Laguna / Navajo country
- four avunculars → rock formation near Acoma
- Wake-like linguistic instability → multilingual local speech, mistranslation, names, references, QR trails, and McDougall/Web3 language

The goal is not to reproduce Joyce's Dublin in costume. The goal is to let the same kinds of structural forces reappear in a different physical medium.

The desert is not the absence of the river. It is where the river has become invisible, directional, geological, and immense.
