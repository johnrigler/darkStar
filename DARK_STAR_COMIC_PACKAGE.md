# Dark Star Comic Package

This note preserves the physical packaging and interaction model for a comic-book / pulp edition of **Dark Star**.

## The sealed comic

The comic-book edition should be distributed **wrapped in clear plastic**, with additional QR-code pages, inserts, sticker sheets, or related physical artifacts sealed behind the visible comic.

The plastic is not merely retail packaging. It creates a physical trust boundary around a specific printed instance.

A sealed copy can contain:

- the visible comic or tract;
- QR-code pages behind it;
- one or more unique QR codes associated with that individual copy;
- sticker sheets or individual labels;
- detachable or perforated interaction pages;
- instructions or ritual fragments that only become available when the package is opened.

The package therefore has at least two states:

**sealed → opened**

The act of opening the package can matter because it changes the trust status of the physical object.

## The pornography / restricted-material echo

The plastic wrapping deliberately recalls the visual language of bagged or restricted adult material: something physically separated from casual handling, especially by children.

Dark Star reverses that association. The protected thing is not primarily obscene content. It can be the reader's **identity material, QR artifacts, stickers, keys, claims, or other unique participation objects**.

The protective gesture therefore becomes strange: instead of shielding a child from forbidden material, the package can be read as shielding a person's identity or participation apparatus from prior handling.

This should remain an allusion rather than an explicit imitation of pornography packaging. The useful element is the cultural memory of the sealed object: private, restricted, intact until opened, and materially different after the seal is broken.

## Physical trust layer

Unique QR codes make the package more than a generic printed copy.

If each instance contains QR material unique to that copy, then the sealed package can function as a primitive physical provenance layer:

**printed instance + unique QR artifact + intact seal**

The plastic does not create cryptographic security. It is only a physical indicator. It can be cut, resealed, substituted, photographed, copied, or discarded. Dark Star should not pretend otherwise.

Its value is that it provides one more layer of evidence in a stack of weak and strong signals:

- physical possession;
- intact or broken packaging;
- unique printed QR material;
- sticker placement;
- cryptographic signatures;
- ledger references;
- checksums or hashes;
- social witnessing.

The plastic is therefore **discardable trust**. A reader may use it as part of the ritual or throw it away and rely on other verification mechanisms.

## Stickers as anchors inside the text

The stickers should not be limited to a final wallet page. The comic itself can contain designated anchor positions where a reader is instructed, invited, or expected to place a sticker.

These anchor positions can be visible, hidden, ambiguous, narrative, or functional.

Examples include:

- a blank square inside a panel;
- an outlined address field;
- a missing word or glyph;
- a speech balloon that becomes complete only after a sticker is applied;
- a map or diagram with an empty anchor point;
- a QR-adjacent marker;
- a place where a physical label links the page to an external ledger object;
- a position whose meaning changes depending on which sticker is used.

This allows the physical copy to become stateful.

Two initially identical comics can diverge after distribution because readers place different unique stickers in different locations.

The result is no longer merely a printed edition. It is a partially user-assembled artifact.

## Identity and agency

The packaging model should preserve the distinction between an **identity artifact** and a centralized identity account.

The object can hand a reader unique material without requiring that a publisher permanently manage the reader's identity.

A sealed QR or sticker can act as an invitation, seed, claim, pointer, public identifier, or other protocol object. Whether it becomes identity depends on what the reader does with it afterward.

The publisher should not be treated as the permanent custodian of that identity.

This extends the existing Dark Star movement:

**reader → holder → opener → assembler → participant**

## Relationship to the perforated ending

The sealed comic package and the perforated ending belong to the same physical grammar.

The reader does not merely turn pages. The reader:

1. receives a sealed object;
2. decides whether to break the seal;
3. encounters unique machine-readable artifacts;
4. removes, tears, or detaches paper elements;
5. places stickers into defined locations;
6. scans or verifies QR material;
7. connects the altered physical object to other people, tools, or ledgers.

The book therefore has a lifecycle rather than a fixed final form.

## Design principle

The comic should make the printed object itself part of the protocol.

The QR code is not just a hyperlink.

The sticker is not just decoration.

The plastic is not just packaging.

The perforation is not just print finishing.

Each element changes what the reader can know, trust, alter, detach, verify, or carry away.

The physical artifact can be completely ignored and discarded, but if the reader chooses to engage with it, the comic becomes an interface between pulp print, local social interaction, cryptographic artifacts, and third-space activity.
