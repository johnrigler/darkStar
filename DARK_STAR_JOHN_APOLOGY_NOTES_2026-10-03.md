## John's apology

John is genuinely apologetic.

The apology is not sarcastic, manipulative, or self-exonerating.

He is not saying:

"I was right and nobody understood me."

He is closer to saying:

"I am sorry this is what I could make."

"I am sorry I could not explain it better."

"I am sorry things did not work the way people expected."

"I built the thing I thought had to be built, without knowing whether the result would be good or bad."

The apology should coexist with conviction.

John still believes the work had to be done.

He simply does not pretend that building it solved everything, that the consequences are known, or that everybody should feel reassured by it.

His tone can hold courtesy, loneliness, responsibility, uncertainty, and the sense that a transition is already underway.

The reader should not mainly pity John.

The apology should make the reader feel that something has happened and that the next part has already begun.
