# Dark Star — John notes — 2026-09-16

John is deliberately different from Matthew, Mark, and Luke. It should feel as if the book has crossed into another narrative regime.

## John and Annie

John appears to be a Catholic priest living with Ann Olivia, whom he calls Annie.

Whether he is really a priest should remain unstable.

He and Annie are clearly together, but almost everything else around their domestic life can shift.

There are many children around him. John repeatedly acts as a judge, interpreter, disciplinarian, or arbiter of the children's world.

The scene should initially support the reader's assumption that these are ordinary children in a crowded domestic environment.

## The bar scene

John later goes to a bar.

Mamalu is there, or the Mamalu figures are there. These are recognizable as Matthew, Mark, and Luke in some sense, though they are not necessarily identical to the chapter figures or to each other.

John is upset with them.

He has had too much to drink and begins yelling at them. The scene should feel accusatory and disordered rather than like a clean theological confrontation.

Then he leaves.

## Children become dogs

After John leaves, the domestic world retrospectively changes.

It begins to seem that there were never any children.

They were dogs.

The book should not resolve whether:

- John misperceived dogs as children;
- the narration lied;
- the children transformed;
- the dogs were always there beneath the human reading;
- John was never a priest;
- the distinction itself has simply ceased to matter.

John is still with Annie. That relationship remains more stable than his office, his authority, or his interpretation of the household.

## Fading out

John and Annie's life does not need a dramatic conclusion.

They simply begin to fade.

Their domestic world loses narrative pressure until they are no longer the center of the book.

The dogs go outside and bark at other dogs.

The reader is then transported into the world of those other dogs.

The transition should work almost as a relay: one creature's attention becomes the doorway into another creature's world.

This anticipates the next major movement, where authorship and genre change again.

## Finnegans Wake behavior

John should be explicitly comfortable in *Finnegans Wake* territory:

- identities recur without remaining identical;
- family roles slide into symbolic roles;
- names behave as echoes;
- domestic scenes become cosmological and then banal again;
- animals and people can exchange interpretive positions;
- the story can fold back on itself without announcing a formal reset.

The goal is not to imitate Joyce sentence-by-sentence. It is to permit the same kind of unstable identity and recursive narrative logic.

## Structural role

John closes the Gospel movement by making it impossible to maintain the earlier distinction between documentary reality, symbolic dream, crowd performance, and domestic fiction.

A rough chapter progression is:

**Matthew: recognizable public world**

**Mark: symbolic machine and attribution failure**

**Luke: mass echo chamber and prolificity**

**John: private world, unstable identity, animals, fading, transition**

John should hand the book off rather than solve it.
