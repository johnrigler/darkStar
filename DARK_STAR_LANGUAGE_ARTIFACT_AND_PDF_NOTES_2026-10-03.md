## Dual legibility

The address-language is not merely opaque encoding. It can remain readable to a human while also remaining valid as a machine object.

Use the term **dual legibility**:

> one artifact is syntactically meaningful to the machine and semantically legible to a human reader.

The index and the utterance can occupy the same artifact.

Luke is the best place to connect this to Charles Taylor's constitutive account of language: new forms of expression can make new distinctions and forms of action available.

## Finkel and archaeological legibility

Irving Finkel provides the material-writing bridge.

The analogy to cuneiform is not that a blockchain is a clay tablet. The useful resemblance is that the substrate shapes the mark, and the mark can outlive the institution that created it.

Distinguish:

- durability: does the object survive?
- recoverability: can its technical meaning be reconstructed?
- legibility: can a human begin recognizing meaning without restoring the entire original software environment?

For the future-reader case, use **archaeological legibility**.

A strong Chisel formulation is:

> Do not merely save the message somewhere. Leave a mark in the thing itself.

Luke should ask:

> What would a stranger make of this object after its author, website, documentation, company, and immediate cultural context were gone?

## Collaboration across time

The phrase "Byzantine generals of language" is evocative, but the more precise mechanism is **diachronic, artifact-mediated collaboration**.

person A -> durable artifact -> person B -> durable artifact -> person C

The participants do not need to know one another, agree with one another, or be alive at the same time.

The artifact becomes the coordination surface.

A plainer Dark Star line:

> We do not have to meet. We have to leave each other enough to continue.

This creates a second success condition beyond mass adoption.

Mass adoption:
many people use it now.

Artifact density:
enough readable artifacts, code, examples, and explanations exist that somebody can rediscover and continue the idea later.

A project can fail as a company and still succeed as technical sediment.

## Conventional PDF

Dark Star needs a conventional PDF that can be read on screen and printed on ordinary full-size paper without requiring the reader to understand booklet imposition.

The PDF should preserve the same substantive information, but it should not pretend to reproduce the intended physical experience.

A good default:

- one composed booklet half-page enlarged and centered on one US Letter or A4 PDF page;
- generous neutral margin around it;
- no reflow into a conventional report;
- preserve typography, page position, side threads, strange language, and illustrations.

Where the physical issue includes sticker packets, tear-outs, QR exercises, or other attached material, the reading PDF should show a photograph, diagram, or representative image of that packet rather than duplicating a large working array of QR codes.

The PDF can state plainly:

> You are not experiencing Dark Star in its intended format.

Then explain why: the intended edition is a printed booklet with physical exercises, scannable or removable objects, and transitions into working systems. The PDF preserves the information and documents the experience, but cannot reproduce every physical interaction.

The asymmetry should create curiosity through fact rather than artificial scarcity.

Do not tell PDF readers that they are less important or unlikely to help. Let the medium communicate that they are observing a designed physical experience rather than receiving the canonical physical packet.

A useful hierarchy:

Dark Star publication system
  -> physical booklet / packet: intended experiential edition
  -> conventional PDF: complete documentary reading edition
  -> HTML / web: active exploratory rendering

The conventional PDF should be easy to quote, archive, inspect, and print.

A separate PDF can remain available for actual duplex booklet printing and folding. These are different outputs and should not be conflated.
