## Language as a technical world

Luke is the best place for the language-animal thread because the chapter is already moving from the hacker's practical question into a different question:

> What kind of world becomes thinkable through the language available to describe it?

The address-language should be presented not merely as encoded English but as a dual-legible artifact: the same object can remain machine-valid and human-readable.

Use the term **dual legibility**:

> one artifact is syntactically meaningful to the machine and semantically legible to a human reader.

The index and the utterance can occupy the same artifact.

A useful contrast is:

opaque encoding:
human language -> machine representation -> software decoding -> human language

dual-legible form:
human language <-> machine-valid artifact

The larger philosophical point is that new forms of expression can make new distinctions and forms of action available.

## Language becomes artifact

Luke can then move into the archaeological question:

> What survives when language becomes an object?

The clay-tablet analogy is useful because the substrate leaves fingerprints on the script, and surviving marks can let later readers reconstruct practices after the original institutions are gone.

Distinguish:

- durability: does the object survive?
- recoverability: can its technical meaning be reconstructed?
- legibility: can a human begin recognizing meaning without restoring the entire original software environment?

Use **archaeological legibility** for the future-reader case.

A useful Chisel formulation is:

> Do not merely save the message somewhere. Leave a mark in the thing itself.

Luke should explicitly ask:

> What would a stranger make of this object after its author, website, documentation, company, and immediate cultural context were gone?

## Three-stage movement

A strong Luke sequence is:

discover the affordance
articulate the affordance
leave enough material evidence for rediscovery

The existing line:

LUKExLUCKxLiCKSxLAKESzzz

should function as evidence rather than decoration. It is readable, constrained, and machine-shaped.

## Collaboration across time

Luke can introduce the idea of **diachronic, artifact-mediated collaboration**.

person A -> durable artifact -> person B -> durable artifact -> person C

The participants do not need to know one another, agree with one another, or be alive at the same time.

The artifact becomes the coordination surface.

A plainer Dark Star line:

> We do not have to meet. We have to leave each other enough to continue.

This creates a second success condition beyond mass adoption.

Mass adoption:
many people use it now.

Artifact density:
enough readable artifacts, code, examples, and explanations exist that somebody can rediscover and continue the idea later.

A project can fail as a company and still succeed as technical sediment.
