# Dark Star — Matthew notes — 2026-09-16

Matthew should be the comparatively legible, documentary world before the later chapters begin to dissolve its assumptions.

## Core dramatic frame

Matthew is about rulers/builders talking to one another while the system is still recognizable as Web2, venture capital, platforms, institutions, founders, wealth, and public argument.

The current poles are:

- Paul Graham as the builder/founder/intellectual-production pole;
- Nick Hanauer as the warning/social-consequence pole, especially the recurring "pitchforks" warning;
- kings/rulers as a broader symbolic class, not necessarily literal monarchs.

This chapter does not need to become a caricature contest. The value is in letting documented statements, essays, talks, interviews, and arguments sit beside each other so the reader can see incompatible assumptions about what is being built and what happens when wealth and institutional power concentrate.

## Relationship to the later Gospel movement

Matthew establishes a world that still appears coherent. Sources can be cited. People have names. Statements have authors. URLs still point somewhere. Web2 still appears to function as a usable historical record.

Mark should then make it feel as if this universe is beginning to fail.

The progression can therefore be read as:

**Matthew: the system can still describe itself**

**Mark: attribution, meaning, and historical memory begin to slip**

**Luke: mass participation arrives, but participation does not restore authenticity**

**John: the previous frame breaks almost completely and the story becomes domestic, Wakean, unstable, and transitional**

## Source behavior

Matthew can support conventional references more directly than Mark. QR codes may point to:

- talks;
- essays;
- archived statements;
- videos;
- interviews;
- Chisel-rendered source records;
- IPFS or ledger-hydrated mirrors where useful.

The chapter can therefore carry argumentative weight that Mark should not. Matthew can say, in effect: **here are the people, here is what they actually said, here are the systems they described or built, and here is the record.**

This makes Matthew partly a Web2 chapter even when its references are being quietly anchored by Web3 machinery.

## Mamalu

Mamalu exists somewhere across Matthew, Mark, and Luke, but those appearances do not need to resolve into a single stable identity. By John, the earlier Mamalu figures can appear together in the bar as if the book has folded several prior roles into one scene.
