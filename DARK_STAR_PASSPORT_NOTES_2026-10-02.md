# Dark Star / Passport Editorial Notes — 2026-10-02

## Dark Star as periodical

Dark Star is best treated as a periodical rather than only a book or tract. That gives it room to publish cultural artifacts, maps, essays, executable links, QR entry points, technical pieces, and recurring features without requiring every idea to fit into one monolithic narrative.

A Zork map is a good example. It can appear as a feature spread inside Dark Star while Lantern remains the actual game interface. The page can function simultaneously as:
- a handmade map;
- an editorial artifact;
- a QR or ledger entry point into Lantern;
- a pointer to interactive-fiction history, tools, preservation projects, and fan work.

The publication can revisit the same artifact across issues instead of trying to exhaust it once.

## Authentic objects rather than reaction layers

A central editorial principle is to point toward culturally substantial objects themselves, not only commentary about those objects.

Examples include:
- old games;
- music videos;
- early-web and Vine-era artifacts;
- handmade maps;
- obscure software;
- zines;
- archival media;
- durable technical or cultural documents.

The surrounding system can connect these objects to commentary, scholarship, history, and related projects, but the object itself remains the center of gravity.

## Passport

**Passport** is a candidate name for a separate kid-facing sibling publication or experience.

It should not simply be "Dark Star for kids." Dark Star can retain its adult tone, death imagery, mythic material, and stranger edges. Passport should have its own visual identity and editorial voice while sharing selected infrastructure.

The name is useful because it naturally supports a physical and digital mechanic:

- visit something;
- place or collect a sticker/stamp;
- record that visit in a personal Passport;
- move on to another artifact, game, place, puzzle, or story.

A physical issue can contain sticker or stamp spaces. A digital Passport can record corresponding discoveries.

The "passport" metaphor should mean a personal record of exploration, not government identity verification.

## Possible Passport content

Passport could emphasize:
- exploration;
- old games and interactive fiction;
- maps;
- puzzles;
- drawing;
- codes and basic cryptography;
- QR-linked artifacts;
- scavenger-hunt style discovery;
- physical stickers and stamps;
- durable messages and records.

Lantern can be one destination inside Passport rather than being renamed or subordinated to Passport.

A child could play Zork, annotate a printed map, scan an issue QR code, collect a sticker, and explore related artifacts without ever needing a wallet or publishing to a ledger.

Any identity, signing, or ledger-writing step should be separate, explicit, and appropriate for parent or guardian involvement rather than being a default part of the kid-facing flow.

## Shared architecture

The projects can remain separate but connected:

- **Dark Star** — adult periodical and cultural/editorial framing
- **Passport** — kid-facing exploration publication/experience
- **Lantern** — interactive-fiction execution layer
- **Chisel** — identity, signing, and ledger publication
- **Mogwai** — discovery and presentation
- **Ledger** — durable address space

This lets Dark Star and Passport both point into Lantern while preserving different editorial identities.

## Serial possibility

Because both Dark Star and Passport can be periodical, neither needs to fully explain or map an artifact in a single issue.

For example:
- one issue introduces Zork I and includes a partial handmade map;
- a later issue revisits it with additional rooms, reader-created maps, unusual routes, or related interactive-fiction projects;
- another issue could move to a different game, music artifact, early-web object, or historical software.

That serial structure turns the publications into guides through a growing cultural address space rather than static catalogs.
