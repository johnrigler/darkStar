## Two PDF outputs

Dark Star should have two distinct PDF forms.

### 1. Reading / archival PDF

This is a conventional PDF for screen reading, quotation, scholarship, ordinary printing, and archiving.

It should preserve the same substantive information as the physical issue, but it should not pretend to reproduce the intended physical experience.

A good default:

- US Letter or A4;
- one composed booklet half-page enlarged and centered on each PDF page;
- generous neutral margin around it;
- no reflow into a normal report;
- preserve typography, page position, side threads, strange language, illustrations, and newspaper structure.

The reader should perceive the booklet page as an object inside the conventional page.

### 2. Duplex booklet PDF

This is the production file for actual double-sided printing, folding, and assembly.

It can retain booklet imposition and the physical mechanics required for the intended edition.

The two outputs should not be conflated.

## Physical packet material

Where the physical issue includes sticker packets, tear-outs, QR exercises, identity material, or other attached objects, the reading PDF should show a photograph, diagram, or representative image of the packet rather than reproduce a large working array of QR codes.

The PDF can state plainly:

> You are not experiencing Dark Star in its intended format.

Then explain the factual reason: the intended edition is a printed booklet with physical exercises, scannable or removable objects, and transitions into working systems. The PDF preserves the information and documents the experience, but cannot reproduce every physical interaction.

This asymmetry should create curiosity through fact, not artificial scarcity.

Do not tell the PDF reader that they are less important or unlikely to help. Let the medium communicate that they are observing a designed physical experience rather than receiving the canonical physical packet.

## Rendering hierarchy

A useful conceptual hierarchy is:

Dark Star publication system
  -> physical booklet / packet: intended experiential edition
  -> conventional PDF: complete documentary reading edition
  -> HTML / web: active exploratory rendering

None of these alone is the ontological container of the work.

The physical packet is the primary designed reading experience.

The conventional PDF is the stable archival and scholarly surface.

The HTML/web version is the active and exploratory rendering.
