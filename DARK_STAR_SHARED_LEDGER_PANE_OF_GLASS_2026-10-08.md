# The Pane of Glass: Writing in One Another's Histories

*Dark Star working essay, 2026-10-08*

## The new medium's message

Take McLuhan's proposal seriously: a medium changes the structure of association, not merely the contents of individual messages. If a public blockchain is a new medium, cryptocurrency's financial application need not exhaust what it communicates. Its distinctive possibility is a shared, durable writing space where strangers can leave artifacts, independently find them, and answer through the same medium.

The point is not that these people share a doctrine. They might disagree entirely. Their practical common ground is that the record can be searched, referenced, and interpreted. That common ground is more significant than either participant's opinions.

## Jack and the pane of glass

The author encountered an independent publisher provisionally called **Jack** through an inscription or message associated with the Bitcoin ABC/eCash ecosystem. The exact chain, transaction, index convention, and Jack's identity still require verification; the encounter is a research lead, not proof of an established relationship.

Imagine Jack looking through one pane of glass: an explorer or application that makes selected blockchain records visible. Chisel is another pane onto overlapping public material. If the author can understand the convention Jack's application indexes, publish a compatible response, and see it rendered in Jack's application, something important has occurred without private coordination.

Jack might never personally notice. The stronger test is whether a third person can reproduce the discovery: find the original artifact, the response, and the relationship between them with independently running tools. That would be interoperability demonstrated, rather than advertised.

## Copying without erasing the trail

Ordinary copying often relocates an idea into another product or institution, where it acquires a new name and appears to have no past. The historical connection may depend on footnotes, interviews, or someone's goodwill.

In a shared ledger, a copy or reply can instead **point directly to the transaction it builds upon**. The new publication becomes another event in a traceable history. Copying a successful convention need not displace its origin; independent implementations can enlarge its usefulness.

This is an affordance, not magic. A blockchain does **not** automatically preserve attribution, authorship, semantic relationships, or availability of every off-chain payload. Those must be expressed through explicit references, signatures where appropriate, shared indexing rules, and durable content storage. History can still be ignored, misread, or intentionally severed in an interface. Different chains and forks can also fragment the common space.

What changes is the cost and possibility of checking the connection. A direct reference can make continuity publicly inspectable rather than a story controlled by a publisher.

## What success looks like

Success need not mean owning a movement, controlling the audience, or converting Jack to a point of view. A modest test is enough:

1. Recover and document Jack's actual published artifact and indexing rule.
2. Construct a response using that rule, preferably with an explicit link to the source transaction.
3. Publish it without requiring Jack's permission or software changes.
4. Observe whether Jack's interface displays it and preserve evidence of the result.
5. Let a third party repeat the lookup and assess what was actually demonstrated.

The distinction from coercive demands for recognition is fundamental. No participant needs to command the world's attention. Each can do a small thing, borrow a useful convention, and leave a public trace for anyone interested enough to follow.

**We are not merely publishing into the same space. We can write into one another's histories.**

The strongest claim isn't that disconnection is impossible. It's that continuity can be made explicit, verifiable, and useful without either party owning the shared medium.
