# Dark Star — Web3 as Character: Jack, the Agent, the Bar, and the Other General — 2026-10-07

## Story purpose

Web3 should increasingly appear in Dark Star not merely as a technical subject but as a character revealed through several narrative levels.

There are at least three layers already implicit in the story:

1. **The bar** — a man is trying to tell the story. People half-listen. They hear pieces, miss pieces, drift away, come back, misunderstand, and occasionally catch something real.
2. **The agent** — someone trained to look for patterns, contacts, networks, threats, and signals is supposed to connect with the narrator or with the system around him.
3. **The hacker** — someone outside the normal institutional channel discovers the same strange affordance independently and begins using it.

These levels should not be collapsed into one exposition.

The story becomes stronger when the reader slowly realizes that all three are observing the same thing from different positions.

## Web3 as a character

Web3 is not simply explained.

It appears through behavior.

It leaves traces.

It is misunderstood by institutions.

It is commercialized by some people, securitized by others, ignored by many, and discovered sideways by hackers.

In this sense Web3 becomes almost a distributed character: nobody owns the full description, but different people encounter fragments of the same thing.

The narrator in the bar talks about it.

The agent tries to classify it.

The hacker uses it.

The ledger remembers all three.

## Jack

Use "Jack" as the provisional story name for the eCash developer/hacker figure unless and until a verified public name is appropriate.

The story does not need to overclaim who Jack is.

The important fact is that the narrator encounters evidence of another technically inclined person independently using cryptocurrency transaction structure as a signaling or publication surface.

The recognition should feel uncanny but technically grounded:

```
I am sending signals into ledgers.

Someone else is sending signals into a ledger.

I did not recruit him.

He did not recruit me.

We arrived at neighboring conclusions from different directions.
```

That is the story event.

Jack does not have to become friend, collaborator, ally, or antagonist.

The idea matters more than the social relationship.

## The other general

The Byzantine Generals metaphor can be used carefully.

Do not make Jack literally a "Byzantine general" in the distributed-systems textbook sense.

Instead, use the image narratively:

> another general has appeared on the far ridge.

The narrator has been behaving as if he were sending messages across a hostile or indifferent landscape with no assurance that anybody meaningful is listening.

Then a signal appears that could only have come from someone who independently noticed the same terrain.

The important emotional movement is:

```
broadcast into apparent emptiness
-> unexpected return signal
-> recognition that the field is inhabited
```

This can echo the Byzantine Generals problem without pretending that the human story is a formal consensus protocol.

A possible recurring image:

```
One general scratches a message into the stone.
Another general, far away, has already done the same.
Neither knew the other was there.
```

## The bar level

At the bar, this should remain ambiguous and almost comic.

The narrator tries to explain:

- the messages in transactions;
- the strange addresses;
- the idea that wallets are hiding most of the ledger;
- the Chinese developer or "Jack";
- the possibility that another hacker is sending signals through eCash;
- the larger claim that Web3 is not what the industry says it is.

The listeners do not fully reject him.

That is important.

They half-listen.

They may ask one practical question, make a joke, change the subject, or nod without following the implication.

This preserves the recurring Dark Star condition:

> the message is not exactly censored; it is socially unable to resolve.

The narrator is not standing before a tribunal.

He is simply unable to make the whole thing legible before attention moves elsewhere.

## The agent level

The agent sees a different problem.

To an intelligence or law-enforcement mindset, unexplained communication channels are not poetic.

They are networks.

Addresses become identifiers.

Transactions become contacts.

Repeated signals become patterns.

A strange GitHub reference inside a transaction is not merely an eccentric message. It is a possible edge in a graph.

The agent therefore understands part of Web3 more quickly than the people at the bar, but through the wrong frame.

The agent asks:

```
Who is talking to whom?
What does this channel enable?
Who controls it?
Can it be attributed?
Can it be stopped?
```

The narrator asks:

```
What if nobody needs to control it?
What if the point is that people can find one another without asking?
```

That difference should create tension without requiring the agent to be stupid or villainous.

## The hacker level

Jack demonstrates the thing rather than explaining it.

That is why the hacker matters.

The bar has speech.

The agent has classification.

The hacker has operational proof.

A transaction exists.

A message exists.

A repository exists.

A pattern exists.

The hacker has crossed the boundary from speculation to artifact.

This allows the story to embody one of Dark Star's recurring rules:

> When Dark Star looks strange, test it.

The narrator can point to the transaction.

The reader can inspect it.

The story leaks into the actual ledger.

## The recognition scene

The encounter with Jack should not be written as destiny or mystical confirmation.

It should be written as independent convergence.

Something like this structurally:

```
The narrator has spent years believing that public ledgers can be used as signals.

He finds an eCash transaction stream containing messages.

The messages point outward toward a developer identity/repository.

The implementation is not the same as Chisel.

The worldview is not necessarily the same.

But the underlying move is recognizable.

Someone else has treated the currency itself as a medium.
```

That is enough to change the narrator's confidence.

Not because Jack validates Chisel.

Because the affordance has been rediscovered independently.

## Web3 becomes plural

This is where Web3 becomes a character rather than a brand.

There is no single Web3.

There is:

- corporate Web3;
- venture-funded Web3;
- wallet Web3;
- token Web3;
- regulator Web3;
- scam Web3;
- hacker Web3;
- the narrator's Web3;
- Jack's Web3;
- the agent's model of Web3.

They occupy the same infrastructure but do not mean the same thing by it.

Dark Star should let these versions collide.

## Relationship to Mogwai

Mogwai supplies the narrator's answer to the problem.

The ledger is almost entirely irrelevant to any one person.

What matters is the ability to detect the tiny set of signals that intersect with one's own identities, addresses, artifacts, and interests.

So the story movement can become:

```
bar:
nobody can quite hear the story

agent:
somebody can see the graph but interprets it as control/problem

hacker:
somebody else independently uses the substrate

Mogwai:
the user builds a local lens that makes the relevant signals visible
```

Mogwai is therefore not just another wallet or explorer.

It is the instrument by which a person recognizes another general on the ridge.

## Relationship to Matthew / Mark / Luke / John

This story layer can echo the four-part structure.

**Matthew**
The hacker notices an affordance before it is institutionally legible.

**Mark**
Power notices that the same affordance can route around conventional control.

**Luke**
Signals multiply, interpretation fragments, spam and relevance become contested, and every observer sees a different network.

**John**
The reader stops relying on the narrator's account and follows the transaction, address, repository, or artifact directly.

The story should eventually make the reader perform the recognition rather than merely hear about it.

## A useful recurring line

> There was another general on the ridge.

It should mean:

not agreement;
not alliance;
not proof of a grand theory;

only that the supposedly empty signaling space was not empty.
