Part One / Gospel / working manuscript notes

Mark

Peter Thiel appears to become a T-Rex dinosaur playing a strange mathematical marble game. The game drifts into German because the page has wandered into Hesse's Das Glasperlenspiel. Nobody announces that a metaphor has begun. The dinosaur simply knows the rules.

Then taxonomy starts arguing with satire. Perhaps the creature is a Tylosaurus Rex connected, by coincidence, to a paleontologist named Thiel. The name itself becomes evidence. That evidence immediately fails because there is already another dinosaur, the Elon Muskiest dinosaur, occupying the naming territory.

Regression

The intellectual dispute keeps moving backward until it seems to have reached dinosaur time. Then the frame slips again. They are children dressed as dinosaurs for Halloween, still arguing, still shooting marbles, still treating the rules as if civilization depends on the outcome.

Das Glasperlenspiel -> paleontology -> nomenclature -> Elon Muskiest dinosaur -> deep time -> Halloween -> marbles The argument survives. Only the dignity of the arguers changes.

Mark should make it impossible to tell whether elite intellectual systems are sophisticated children's games, or whether children's games are the primitive form of every elite intellectual system.

Marcus Lyons / Egyptus

In Mark the person of Matthew becomes a lion. Marcus Lyons; Egyptus; a lion walking out of the unstable language of Finnegans Wake. A human founder has become a heraldic species, an animal speaking as though its dominance were a law of nature.

The lion declares that the true capitalist is powerful enough not to compete. Competition is for losers. The line echoes the anti-competition philosophy associated with Peter Thiel, but here it is pushed to its most alpha, almost zoological conclusion: victory means escaping competition altogether, ideally by owning the ground on which everyone else must play.

The marble game now looks less innocent. Its elaborate rules are not merely a test of cleverness; they can turn into a system for choosing the permanent winner. The lion calls that system wisdom. The children dressed as dinosaurs continue playing.
