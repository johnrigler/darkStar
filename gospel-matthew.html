Matthew

Matthew is a fictional composite made from Nick Hanauer and Paul Graham. The point is not to hide either source. The collision is the character: a voice worried about concentrated wealth sharing a skull with a voice that wants small groups of builders to ignore institutional permission and make things.

He can contradict himself without noticing. One paragraph can sound like a warning about plutocracy; the next can sound like a startup essay explaining why the clever people should leave the institution behind. The contradiction should remain productive rather than being reconciled by the narrator.

The Matthew problem

If capital finances the bazaar, when does it become the landlord? If the builder removes a gate, what keeps the builder from erecting a new gate around the interface? If a protocol is genuinely permissionless, what exactly is left for an investor to own?

working movement: wealth -> access -> permission -> founder -> gate -> protocol Do not finish with a thesis. Let the abstraction harden into a king. Let the king acquire teeth.
