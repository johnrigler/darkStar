John Rigler

Systems hacker

Author bio · Updated 22 October 2026

John Rigler is a systems hacker and writer with a background in enterprise systems administration. His work examines how systems behave, what they make difficult, and which assumptions can be tested or changed.

Software, records, and institutional processes

His software work includes Chisel and related tools for portable identity, public records, and publication that can be reconstructed independently of the service that first presented it. In the Commons, he applies the same approach to credit reporting, collection, and court workflows: follow the inputs, the permitted actions, the bottlenecks, and the surviving record.

Water, clay, and spaces for living

His interests extend beyond information technology into water conservation and the use of locally available clay. He is exploring spaces that connect indoors and outdoors, where shelter, growing, shade, heating, and cooling can be considered as parts of one system. The questions concern how materials, water, sun, and daily use work together.

Beautiful Dark Star connects this work through essays, fiction, physical artifacts, and experiments. The Commons portal argument shows the method applied to institutions that control both a disputed record and the channel for challenging it.
