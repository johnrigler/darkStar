# Johnny Eaglefeather: Bird of Flames

*A Dark Star narrative fragment. First-person character voice, not biographical explanation.*

They called me Johnny McDougall.

They called me Johnny Ravenfeather.

And Johnny Ravenfeather flew too close to the sun.

That's how they tell it, anyway. As if the story ended there. As if the only thing a bird can do after meeting the sun is fall.

But I came back burning.

I came down into a dark world, a bird of flames. Not carrying a lantern. Not explaining what the light meant. I was on fire.

And now they call me Johnny Eaglefeather.

The fourth avuncular figure. The eagle. The old uncle in the Wakean universe, circling back through somebody else's mouth. I say *I*, and perhaps it is me. Perhaps it is the character speaking. There's no particular place where one stops and the other begins.

So I come into the room and tell them where I've been.

Somebody hears a myth. Somebody hears a song. Somebody remembers **“Bird of Flames.”** Somebody hears only another man talking.

Anywho, I'm still talking.

---

## Editorial notes

- This is a **performed first-person passage**: the speaker becomes Johnny Eaglefeather in the telling. Keep that ambiguity; don't flatten it into a character encyclopedia entry.
- The names **Johnny McDougall**, **Johnny Ravenfeather**, and **Johnny Eaglefeather** are sequential masks / transformations; their exact relationships can develop in later episodes.
- **Avuncular** is the intended term, not “Uncular.” The fourth figure corresponds symbolically to the eagle associated with John in the four evangelists; this is a literary mapping, not a historical assertion.
- **“Bird of Flames”** is a song reference supplied by the author. Artist and exact source remain to be identified; avoid assuming a particular recording.
- The flight toward the sun invokes Icarus, while the flaming descent gestures toward a phoenix; neither needs to be spelled out inside the character's dialogue.
- This fragment belongs to the layered *Dark Star* narrative, and can be relocated into the bar-story / storyteller framing without losing its first-person voice.

## Raven Proxy / The Egg

They called me Johnny Raven Proxy, too. Johnny McDougall. John Edward McDew-Dashgal, two E, dash dash E. Give him another title. Doctor. Reverend. Counselor. Lawyer. All the lawyer titles, all the letters after the name. Three nine six nine two. Keep writing. Eventually the name takes up the whole line.

Then there's the Macintosh version of me. Hold down Option and type J, M, C, D. The letters escape. The first one looks almost like a triangle, maybe a lambda. The others are whatever the keyboard says they are. Nobody in the room can agree whether this is a name or the machinery underneath a name.

You print a page in black and then a word appears in red.

Nothing has changed except the color, and everything has changed. You have stepped outside the black type. The word is another signal. The escape is part of the language.

Johnny Raven Coin. Johnny Raven Proxy. The software that runs on the coin. You can send it somewhere and there he is, another bird, another proxy, another name.

But the crow flies too close to the sun. Pride burns him, and the fall is the end of the lesson. That's not my flight.

They called me Johnny Eaglefeather in Yakima. Here, anyway, I can become him.

In the dark I see the older bird, its clothing frayed, tending an egg in the nest. The bird has been there longer than the room. Longer than the story. And the egg begins to move.

Then the egg launches.

She comes through it, the new woman, glitching into the darkness. The bird of flames is burning as she arrives. The old bird has given everything it could to make this passage possible.

That's the difference. The crow was burned by pride. This bird burns on purpose. Its fire is the light by which the new world can be seen.

And somebody asks which Johnny is speaking.

All of them, I suppose.

### Working notes on this variation

- **Johnny Raven Proxy / Johnny Raven Coin** links the character to the software that runs on or interfaces with the coin. This is a literary and technical doubling, not a claim that executable software literally runs inside a UTXO.
- **John Edward McDew-Dashgal, 2E / --E** is kept as orally supplied spelling and punctuation, awaiting an authoritative written rendering.
- **39692** is a recurring identifying number in the character's title-heavy persona.
- **Option-J/M/C/D** refers to Macintosh Option-key typographic transformations; exact glyphs are not fixed here because they depend on keyboard layout. The intended motif is escaping ordinary black-type language into another signaling channel.
- **Yakima / Johnny Eaglefeather** is part of the narrator's recounted naming experience; do not generalize the naming convention to Yakima people or anyone named John.
- **Crow versus bird of flames:** the crow's Icarian burning is pride and punishment; the bird of flames' burning is voluntary sacrifice and illumination.
- **The older bird and the egg:** an image inspired by the author's account of Lynch's visual language. The egg's launch introduces a new woman glitching into darkness; the bird's self-sacrifice makes her arrival legible.
