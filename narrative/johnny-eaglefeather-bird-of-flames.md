# Johnny Eaglefeather: Bird of Flames

*A Dark Star narrative fragment. First-person character voice, not biographical explanation.*

They called me Johnny McDougall.

They called me Johnny Ravenfeather.

And Johnny Ravenfeather flew too close to the sun.

That's how they tell it, anyway. As if the story ended there. As if the only thing a bird can do after meeting the sun is fall.

But I came back burning.

I came down into a dark world, a bird of flames. Not carrying a lantern. Not explaining what the light meant. I was on fire.

And now they call me Johnny Eaglefeather.

The fourth avuncular figure. The eagle. The old uncle in the Wakean universe, circling back through somebody else's mouth. I say *I*, and perhaps it is me. Perhaps it is the character speaking. There's no particular place where one stops and the other begins.

So I come into the room and tell them where I've been.

Somebody hears a myth. Somebody hears a song. Somebody remembers **“Bird of Flames.”** Somebody hears only another man talking.

Anywho, I'm still talking.

## Editorial notes

- This is a **performed first-person passage**: the speaker becomes Johnny Eaglefeather in the telling. Keep that ambiguity; don't flatten it into a character encyclopedia entry.
- The names **Johnny McDougall**, **Johnny Ravenfeather**, and **Johnny Eaglefeather** are sequential masks / transformations; their exact relationships can develop in later episodes.
- **Avuncular** is the intended term, not “Uncular.” The fourth figure corresponds symbolically to the eagle associated with John in the four evangelists; this is a literary mapping, not a historical assertion.
- **“Bird of Flames”** is a song reference supplied by the author. Artist and exact source remain to be identified; avoid assuming a particular recording.
- The flight toward the sun invokes Icarus, while the flaming descent gestures toward a phoenix; neither needs to be spelled out inside the character's dialogue.
- This fragment belongs to the layered *Dark Star* narrative, and can be relocated into the bar-story / storyteller framing without losing its first-person voice.
