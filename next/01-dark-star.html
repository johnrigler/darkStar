Dark Star: the interval

Dark Star is not primarily a prediction of collapse. It is a name for an interval in which an old arrangement remains powerful after some of its assumptions have stopped fitting the world beneath it.

The practical question is narrower than apocalypse:

What continues to work when assumptions disappear?

If a domain disappears, can an identifier still be recovered? If a company closes, can a signature still verify? If an explorer goes offline, can another indexer reconstruct the record? If a professional boundary becomes optional, can a person still act without pretending to possess every specialist's expertise?

Dark Star treats cryptographic keys, signatures, public ledgers, content hashes, static source, paper artifacts, and simple protocols as experiments in continuity.

The name is Saturnian because the project is interested in time, limits, succession, recurrence, winter, memory, and reversal. That mythology is a map, not proof. The engineering claims must survive without it.

The book therefore has two obligations: be strange enough to open another way of seeing, and exact enough that the reader can test what it says.
