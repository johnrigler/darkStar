The public index

Institutions exercise power not only by creating records, but by controlling where records live and how they can be found.

A newspaper owns an archive. A court depends on a docket system. A marketplace owns seller history. A platform owns the account and search interface.

Chisel explores a narrower alternative: use deliberately constructed, human-readable cryptocurrency addresses as durable public coordinates.

readable address
      ↓
public coordinate
      ↓
ordered transactions
      ↓
recoverable artifacts

The chain does not decide what the coordinate means. It preserves valid transactions and ordering. A published grammar and independent readers supply interpretation.

The strong claim is therefore not "the blockchain is truth." It is:

No institution needs to remain the sole surviving source of the record it asks everyone else to trust.

A durable index still needs signatures, revision rules, filters, privacy boundaries, and interpreters. Power does not disappear. Custody and interpretation become separable.
