Recombined agency

C. P. Snow described two intellectual cultures. Modern professional life has produced many more: law, science, software, security, cloud operations, finance, compliance, design, publishing, medicine, management, and dozens of sub-specialties inside each.

Those divisions often represent real expertise. They can also become boundaries around who is permitted to act.

AI changes the cost of crossing those boundaries.

Expertise can remain specialized while agency becomes general.

A person does not become a lawyer, historian, programmer, designer, editor, cryptographer, and network engineer merely by using a model. But a person with judgment and enough technical context can move among those bodies of knowledge without assembling a permanent department for every transition.

This is not "everyone can do everything." It is a change in coordination cost.

The resulting question applies beyond software: which handoffs still protect something real, and which handoffs exist because an older information environment made them unavoidable?
