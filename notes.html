Welcome to "Dark Star". This is where I will collect my thoughts. I am the narrarator of this thing. But I don't really want to be. I distrust most fiction. I think it lets us trick ourselves into believing that we are embarking on some sort of quest and will pop out the other side being wiser, stronger, better for the journey. I believe that fiction acts as a proxy for our own action. If we read it, we appear to not be moved to action, but rather somehow come to believe that we took action. So I don't like fiction. I may be autistic. I have always felt as if I was missing some instruction that the other kids got. But I think their lives were simply easier because they just didn't ask questions. To me this was more storytelling. Does it not rot your brain? I think instead of storytelling, perhaps I rely instead on something more mythical or lyrical. The texts that really draw me in seem to be apocalyptic. Dark Star (or Ann Olivia) are the names of the novels that float around in my head. Ann Olivia is a reference to "Anna Livia" from Finnegans Wake. This was James Joyce's largely forgotten "night novel" book-end to Ulysses. I am also quite a fan of David Lynch movies. "Dark Star" is a zombie story. It will start in a foreign world that appears to be a mirror of ours with clear racial character-swapping. --- - a black middle class scientist - white natives with voodoo rituals - an exotic and sexually coded "natives" Then this backstory will begin to morph. It won't be coded racially anymore, but will now begin to open up as a story with hundreds of characters and mass confusion. --- - middle part is clearly different - we don't remember how we got here - something else becomes vital Next is a very specific scene with all new characters. --- - liberal arts students at a bar - entitled intellectuals - "Annie" is one of them, reading - other student has gotten some LSD - Annie takes some - Then the lights turn on and adults take her away --- - Annie travels by car, bus, train, and boat - Outside she sees death and chaos - She is always safe - Eventually goes to sleep on a boat. --- - Wakes up on an Airplane - Appears to have been dreaming That is it. No explanation, no redemption, no character arc. No proxy for the readers own action. The book would be unsettling on purpose. So remember when I said that I don't really trust fiction? This is my version. My focus then goes to presentation. Every letter centered on the page in an experimental style. QR Codes would go to into Mogwai|Dark Star. The tension can only be relieved by actual participation in Dark Star--- person to person ledger interaction.
