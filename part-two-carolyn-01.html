Part Two / different voice / working manuscript notes

Carolyn Fowler

The second movement changes palette, voice and geography. It is a women's-novel-like section in the New Mexico desert: Grants, the Continental Divide, Navajo country, Laguna, Acoma, roads, mesas, truck stops, weather, distance. It is not narrated as a continuation of John's Gospel voice.

The Washers-at-the-Ford structure is displaced into the desert. The river becomes the Continental Divide: a place where water is separated by an invisible decision about which ocean it would reach. The women can stand near one another while arguing from incompatible maps.

The argument is the puzzle

Native women argue across cultural and linguistic boundaries. The reader has to infer what is actually disputed, what has been translated badly, what is a joke, what belongs to a different frame, and whether a single correct resolution even exists.

Finnegans Wake in the desert. No river in front of them. The divide is conceptual, geographic, linguistic. Do not invent tribal facts to make the symbolism convenient. Where specific traditions matter, source them.

Carolyn Fowler may appear inside the Carolyn Fowler book. A women's book group can discuss the book while the reader is reading it. The recursion should become interesting enough to feel dangerous, then be interrupted before it closes perfectly.
